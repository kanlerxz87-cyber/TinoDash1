Black Swan — real-world economic conditions

Growth and inflation are never consistent over 50 years. This section mimics real conditions with historical stress cases and tests the phased-investing path: park sale proceeds in 3-month–2-year Treasuries for 24–36 months after inheritance, then phase into stocks — to test how it softens 1929 / 2008-style crash-entry risk.

Phased-investing test: lump-sum vs. Treasury parking

ScenarioLump-sum stocks at salePhased (Treasuries 24–36 mo → stocks)Winner
Crash (−40%) in year 1 of parkingBuys the peak; deep drawdownBuys after the fall at lower pricesPhased
Steady rise (+10%/yr) during parkingFully invested in the riseBuys higher later; trails lump-sumLump-sum — reinvestment risk

Rate shock (stocks & bonds fall)Hit on stocksShort bills mature at par; reinvest at higher yieldsPhased
Reinvestment risk, stated plainly: phasing trades crash-entry risk for the risk that markets rise while you wait. Over 24–36 months the expected cost is roughly the stock–Treasury return gap on the uninvested balance. The household must pre-commit (see Questions) how much crash protection it is willing to pay for — before knowing which scenario occurs.

Treasury parking — how it works

  • Instruments: 3-month, 6-month, 1-year, and 2-year Treasury bills/notes — laddered so a tranche matures every 3–6 months across the 24–36 month window.
  • Why short: short maturities mature at par regardless of rate moves; long bonds fell in 2022. State-tax-exempt interest (federal only) — verify current treatment with CPA.
  • Yields are live, not fixed: check Treasury FiscalData / TreasuryDirect at the time of sale. The model defaults to 4.3% — a placeholder, not a quote (flagged F-10).
  • Investment policy first: write the policy (targets, ladder, rebalancing, tax lots) before proceeds arrive — see Questions Q-12 and Taxes.

50-year baseline — why smooth projections mislead

Baseline projections in this site use ranges and regimes, not a single growth rate:

  • Inflation regimes: 2–3% baseline, with 5–12% stagflation regimes (1970s) and deflation regimes (1930s) in the simulation.
  • Housing: Bay Area homes have grown faster than inflation long-term but with deep drawdowns (1990–96, 2008–11). The model tests 0–7%/yr.
  • Stocks: long-run ~7–10% nominal with −30% to −60% crash years. The distribution chart on the Model page embeds these regimes.
  • Review annually: extreme scenarios are re-run every year with updated inputs (see Suggestions S-04).
