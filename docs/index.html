Inherited Property Decision Dashboard

Privacy-first planning workspace for two anonymized California properties: Property A (Cupertino, Santa Clara County; planned to be inherited by trust in a future window) and Property B (San Francisco; already owned by the same future inheritor). This dashboard organizes assumptions, official-source checks, projections, risks, and open questions. It is decision support, not legal/tax/investment advice.

What would change the answer (read first):
  • Property A occupancy/Prop 19 eligibility: If the heir makes Property A their primary residence within 1 year of transfer and files required claims, parent-child exclusion may partially/fully limit reassessment; if not, a market-value reassessment can dominate the outcome.
  • Transfer timing and basis: A sale in the same tax year as inheritance, trust basis rules, and whether a step-up in basis applies can materially change income/capital-gain tax.
  • Actual market rent and vacancy: The rent path is weakly supported without independent Cupertino comps; user-provided utility costs and voucher/rent assumptions can flip the ranking.
  • Estate/liquidity size: If total estate exceeds federal exclusion or has illiquid assets, forced-sale or tax-planning constraints may override “maximize profit” projections.
  • Property B lease/legal constraints: SF rent control, Prop H/tenant rules, unit legality, and insurance/maintenance realities can change net cash flow and risk.
  • Rates and returns: Treasury yields, mortgage/borrowing costs, inflation, insurance, capital expenditures, and equity returns (especially sequence-of-returns after sale) can change the 5/10/30-year ranking.

Executive Recommendation (Preliminary, Conditional)

Conditional / needs validation

Based on current user-supplied inputs and general official rules, no irreversible path should be selected now. The preferred path is a decision-gated process: confirm legal/tax eligibility and real valuations first, then compare mixed paths. If Prop 19 primary-residence exclusion is clearly available and the heir wants to live in Property A, a live-first or live-then-sell path may be attractive; if not eligible and independent comps support strong rent, rent-with-reserves may compete; if liquidity/diversification is the priority, a sell-and-phased-invest (Treasury ladder then DCA into diversified portfolio) path reduces crash-entry timing risk relative to lump-sum investing immediately after sale.

This is not a final recommendation because key inputs (Prop 19 eligibility, date-of-death value, rent comps, estate size, trust terms, insurance/capex, lease/local rules) are unverified or missing.
