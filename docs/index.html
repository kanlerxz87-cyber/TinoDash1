Cupertino & San Francisco House Inheritance Decision Analysis

A privacy-first, source-linked decision workspace for an anticipated inheritance of Property A (Cupertino, Santa Clara County, CA) and the existing ownership of Property B (San Francisco, CA). All figures are rounded to the nearest $10, labeled as estimates/ranges where unverified, and linked to official sources where possible.

Privacy notice: This site intentionally omits names, exact addresses, ZIP codes, parcel/APN identifiers, and unrounded values. Raw user-provided figures were rounded to the nearest $10 for display. Do not add personal identifiers to this repository or site.
Not legal/tax/financial advice. This dashboard organizes public rules, assumptions, and decision questions. Before any action, confirm with a California estate attorney, CPA/tax adviser, property-tax professional, and fiduciary financial adviser using private, verified documents.

What Would Change the Answer (Read First)

Factors that can flip the recommendation

  • Prop 19 eligibility: If an heir occupies Property A as a primary residence within 1 year and files required claims, parent-child property-tax base exclusion may partially apply. If not, full market-value reassessment is likely. High impact
  • Date-of-death value & basis: The stepped-up basis, capital gain exposure, and estate tax exposure depend on private date-of-death valuations and actual estate inventory. High impact
  • Estate size vs. federal exemption: Federal estate/gift exclusion is $15,000,000 per individual in 2026 (IRS), inflation-adjusted after 2026; portability/prior gifts/change in law can alter exposure. High impact
  • Sale timing: A sale in the same tax year as inheritance can change income/capital-gain outcomes; basis, holding period, and state tax must be modeled before action. High impact
  • Actual rents/costs: Property A rent is currently unverified; using a placeholder rent can make "rent" look better/worse than reality. High impact
  • Liquidity needs: If the estate needs cash for taxes, debt, repairs, or equalization, forced-sale risk can dominate long-run return optimization. High impact

Decision rule (agree before figures arrive)

Pre-commitment: The household should agree in writing, before final private valuations arrive, which path wins under which conditions. This prevents reverse-engineering the "best" option from one favorable projection.
