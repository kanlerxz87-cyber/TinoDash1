Black Swan & Stress Tests

Stress scenarios for inconsistent growth, inflation, rate shocks, and crash-entry timing. These are illustrative stress tests, not forecasts.

Key point: A sell decision can look good on average returns but fail if proceeds are invested right before a major drawdown or if inflation/rates/capex spike. The phased investing path exists to test and reduce that timing risk.

Phased investing path (requested)

Design: After Property A sale, park proceeds in a 3-month to 2-year Treasury ladder for 24–36 months, then phase into diversified equities (and/or balanced allocation) on a pre-written schedule. This is compared against immediate lump-sum equity investment.

FeatureLump sumPhased Treasury ladder + DCADecision note
Entry timing riskHighLowerPhasing reduces regret from 1929/2008-like entry points.
Cash drag / missed upsideLowMediumIf markets rally immediately, phased entry underperforms.
LiquidityMedium (depends on allocation)High during ladder periodUseful if estate/capex/tax liabilities uncertain.
Reinvestment riskMarket entry concentratedYield/rate reinvestment during ladder; equity entry spread outShow both risks side by side.
Operational complexityLowMediumRequires IPS, account setup, tax-aware rebalancing.
Best forLong horizon, high risk tolerance, disciplined investorLarge lump sum after sale, sequence-risk concern, need for optionalityCan be blended (e.g., partial lump sum + partial DCA).

Annual review triggers

  • Re-run model annually and after major law/rate/insurance/valuation changes.
  • Review extreme scenarios annually: equity drawdown >30%, mortgage/risk-free rate +/-2%, insurance +50%, major capex event, vacancy >10%.
  • Track proposed legislation separately from enacted law; do not change decisions on proposals alone.
