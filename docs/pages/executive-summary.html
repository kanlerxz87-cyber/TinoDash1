Overview

Executive Summary

A decision-ready summary of the Property A (Cupertino, inherited by trust, target 2040-2050) vs. Property B (San Francisco, already owned) planning question. This summary is intentionally conditional: no final path should be selected until private facts are verified.

Bottom line: There is no defensible "best" path yet. The answer changes materially based on Prop 19 eligibility, date-of-death valuation, actual rent, estate liquidity, tax year of sale, and the household's pre-committed decision rule.

Situation

Assets (anonymized)

  • Property A: Cupertino, Santa Clara County, CA. Expected to be inherited by trust around 2040-2050. Estimated value ~ $3,100,000 (Nov 2026, user estimate, rounded, unverified). Historically low Prop 13 assessed value relative to market.
  • Property B: San Francisco, CA. Already owned by the prospective inheritor. Estimated value ~ $1,800,000 (Nov 2026, user estimate, rounded, unverified). 3 units: 2 rented, 1 owner-occupiable; ~ $7,150/mo rent (user-stated, rounded).
  • Liquid inheritance: "Considerable amount of money" may be included; amount unknown. Federal estate tax exposure depends on total taxable estate, prior gifts, deductions, and portability.

Decision to make

After inheriting Property A, should the heir:

  • Live in it (primary residence, possible Prop 19 benefit)?
  • Rent it (investment property, reassessment/landlord/tax implications)?
  • Sell it and reinvest (stocks/Treasuries/other), with reinvestment risk managed?
  • Use a mixed/phased path (live-then-sell, rent-then-sell, sell-and-phase into markets)?

Conditional Recommendation

If Prop 19 eligible & heir truly wants to live in A

Favor live-in or live-then-sell, after confirming long-term occupancy, exclusion filing, liquidity, and imputed-rent value. This can preserve a low assessed base but concentrates wealth in one asset.

If Prop 19 not eligible & diversification/liquidity needed

Favor sell-and-phased-invest or rent-then-sell, depending on after-tax proceeds, rent evidence, and readiness. Do not lump-sum invest without an IPS and crash-entry plan.

If evidence is incomplete

Favor no irreversible decision yet. Complete the Questions & Missing Info checklist, get private valuations, and run the pre-committed Decision Framework.

Critical Missing Information

  • Date-of-death FMV appraisal for Property A and full estate inventory.
  • Trust terms, distribution powers, debt/liens, and any lease restrictions.
  • Independent market rent and insurance quotes for Property A; verified Property B lease/rent-control status.
  • Heir's tax profile, filing status, other income, residency plans, and liquidity needs.
  • Property A TRA/published tax rate and Prop 19 eligibility facts.
