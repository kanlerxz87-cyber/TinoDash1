Executive summary — important details

  • Two different assets: A is inherited (step-up basis at date of death; Prop 19 reassessment rules apply). B is already owned (existing basis, existing tenants, rent control may apply). They are never treated as the same.
  • California has no estate/inheritance tax. Federal estate tax only above the exclusion ($13,990,000 in 2025; $15,000,000 in 2026 per Rev. Proc. 2025-32). Income-tax basis, estate-tax value, and property-tax assessment are three separate numbers — see Taxes.
  • Same-year sale is the tax-cleanest exit: step-up basis ≈ sale price, so gain ≈ $0 (minus selling costs). Delaying the sale creates gain, depreciation, and recapture.
  • Prop 19 is occupancy-conditional: claim only if the heir occupies A long-term (move in within 1 year; file BOE-19-P within 3 years; BOE-266 within 1 year). Cap: factored base + $1,044,586 (2025–2027).
  • Property tax is the swing cost: the live path keeps the factored base plus only the excess over the Prop 19 allowance; the rent path reassesses to market. At a $3,100,000 market value the year-1 gap is about $12,120/yr at the typical 1.16% rate and about $13,890/yr at the 1.33% rate the owner's own bills imply. Live-path tax ≈ $23,840–$27,340/yr; rent-path tax ≈ $36,000–$41,000/yr.
  • Insurance is unverified: the ~$1,500/yr figures are user guesses. No official premium exists — quotes are required before any path is ranked on cost (see Flagged).
  • Rent for A is a placeholder pending comparables — the rent path is a floor until independent estimates arrive.
  • Nothing here is a recommendation yet. The model ranks paths under stated ranges; the household's pre-committed criteria (Questions page) decide. Re-run annually with updated inputs.
