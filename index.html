Executive summary

What should the household do with Property A?

Property A (Cupertino 95014, est. $3,100,000) passes to the heir through a trust around 2040–2050. Property B (San Francisco 94122, est. $1,800,000) is already owned by the heir: three units, two let at about $7,150/mo, one occupiable. The question is whether to live in A, rent A, or sell A and reinvest — after estate tax, income tax, property tax, insurance, utilities, maintenance, and liquidity.

The one-line answer. On the model's base assumptions, selling Property A in the inheritance year and investing the proceeds ends ahead of keeping it — about $23,413,400 of net wealth after 20 years, versus $17,187,640 for living in it and $13,804,840 for renting it. But the margin is assumption-driven, not proven: both insurance and market rent for A are still blank. No path should be chosen until those are filled in.

What would change the answer

1 · Prop 19 occupancy
Occupying A within one year keeps the factored base plus only the excess over the $1,044,586 allowance (2025–2027). Renting it out triggers full reassessment to market. Source: BOE Prop 19.
2 · Same-year sale vs hold
Selling in the year of death captures the §1014 step-up, so gain is approximately zero. Every year of delay adds gain, and renting adds 25% §1250 recapture. Sources: IRS.
3 · Home appreciation
If Property A appreciates faster than the equity assumption, holding wins. At the model's base 4.0%/yr house growth against 7.5%/yr equities, it does not. Change the slider on What-Ifs and watch the ranking.
4 · Federal estate tax
$15,000,000 per person in 2026, 40% above it, Form 706 due in 9 months. California has no estate or inheritance tax. Source: IRS · Estate vs basis vs assessment.
Reinvestment risk, stated next to the headline. Phasing sale proceeds through 24–36 months of Treasuries reduces the chance of buying at a crash peak — it does not remove risk, it swaps crash-entry risk for the risk that markets rise while the money waits. In the 20-year base comparison the phased path ($22,191,860) trails lump-sum ($23,413,400) by about $1,221,540 precisely because no crash occurred in the base case. See Black-Swan & phased investing.
