Black Swan Lab · Real crashes re-priced on Property A's proceeds

Growth and inflation are never consistent — so model them that way

Two jobs: (1) show what happened to real portfolios in the great crashes, (2) test the phased-investing path (parking net proceeds in 3M–2Y Treasuries for 24–36 months, then phasing into equities) against lump-sum entry specifically for a Property A sale in the same tax year as death. 2) Sequence of returns — why the same average return destroys one heir and not another

Arithmetic that matters: a −50% drawdown needs +100% to recover. $7.4M entered at a top → $3.7M → needs $7.4M again. A ladder earning ≈ 4.4–4.7% while equities fall 30% means the ladder tranche buys equities ≈ 30% cheaper: on a $7.4M pool phased over 30 months through a GFC-style path, the modeled end-value advantage vs lump-sum is ≈ +$700,000–$1,100,000 at the horizon (see Projections with the 2008 preset). Conversely, in a straight-up market, lump-sum beats the ladder by roughly the drag of the parked yield differential (≈ 1–3%/yr for 2–3 yrs ≈ $150,000–$600,000). The ladder pays for itself exactly when the sale happens near a top — which is unknowable in advance. That asymmetry is the entire argument.

3) The phased-investing path, mechanically (the deliverable you asked for)

  1. Day 0 (sale closes, same tax year as death): 100% of net proceeds → laddered Treasuries: e.g., 25% 3-month, 25% 6-month, 25% 1-year, 25% 2-year (current yields 4.36/4.45/4.59/4.63% — 2026-11-24). No equities yet.
  2. Months 7–36: every 3 months, each maturing rung rolls into the longest remaining rung; starting month 13, begin phase-in: 1/24th of the equity target per month (or 1/36th if crash preset active and economy deteriorating — discretion belongs to the IPS, not to headlines).
  3. End of month 24–36: 100% of the equity sleeve invested at average prices across the window; bond sleeve exhausted; policy weights locked; bands enforced.
  4. Reinvestment-risk guardrails: if yields fall > 150 bp before phase-in completes, the IPS pre-authorizes extending ladder duration to 3 years (not beyond) — captured in IPS §4.
  5. Tax treatment: Treasury interest = federal ordinary income, CA/local exempt. No capital gains until equities are bought/sold. Dividends/cap gains after phase-in taxed as usual (15/20% + 3.8% NIIT; CA ordinary rates).
  6. Estate/liquidity note: during the ladder window, keep 6 months of household spend in the ladder’s shortest rung — do not sell rungs early for liquidity.

5) Modeling real growth + inflation over 50 years (not consistent — on purpose)

  • Regime approach, not a single CAGR: the projections in this site compound appreciation at 3%/5%/7% and inflation at 1%/2.5%/5%+ — not one blended line. Real purchasing power is shown alongside nominal where it matters.
  • Historical regimes used as anchors: 1950–65 disinflationary boom; 1966–82 Great Inflation (CPI > 10% peak 1980); 1982–2000 disinflation bull; 2000–09 lost decade; 2009–21 ZIRP bull; 2021–26 re-inflation + rate regime. A 50-year plan must survive at least one full cycle of each.
  • Property tax follows FMV at death, not growth rates — the 2045 transfer resets the base to FMV (unless Prop 19). This single fact dominates rent-vs-sell math and is independent of which growth regime arrives.
  • Dividends + buybacks ≈ 1.5–2.5%/yr of total return historically; both taxed when paid/realized — the model uses 6.5% nominal total return (below the post-2009 mean, above the 1929–2009 mean) and states it.
  • Tail scenarios the plan must survive: (i) 1929: deflation + −89% DJIA; (ii) 1973–74: stagflation + −48% S&P; (iii) 1987: −34% one quarter; (iv) 2000–02: −49%; (v) 2008: −57%; (vi) 2022: stock/bond joint drawdown. Every one is already priced into the ladder design and the IPS bands.

6) Black-swan checklist (review annually)

  • Re-price the equity sleeve: does end-of-window allocation still match the IPS risk band?
  • Re-check the ladder: are maturity rungs still in place? Any rung tapped early (policy breach)?
  • Re-check inflation: CPI trend vs assumed 2.5%; if > 4% for 6+ months, raise the equity/TIPS mix per IPS §5.
  • Re-check sequence: would a −30% drop in year 1 of phase-in break the plan’s liquidity floor? (No — 6-mo spend stays in bills.)
  • Re-check taxes: Treasury interest exemption still in force at CA/federal? Forms 1099-INT harvested for basis?
  • Re-check law: estate exclusion indexation (2027), AB 1482 (2030 sunset), SF/Prop H bill, Prop 19 allowance re-index (2027-02-16).
  • Document any deviation in the decision log with date + reason — Suggestions Review Board.
Bottom line: the phased Treasury-ladder path does not predict crashes — it underwrites them. If the inheritance lands in 2040–2050, no model run today can know the tape then; the ladder is the only structure that is indifferent to whether the market gaps −30% the week after the first tranche is due to rotate.
