Proposition 19 · RTC §63.2 · BOE-19-P · The inheritance property-tax rule

Proposition 19, deconstructed — eligibility first, math second

Prop 19 (Nov 3, 2020) replaced Props 58/193. It is the single most misunderstood rule in inherited-property planning. Official hub: California State Board of Equalization — Proposition 19. Statute: Rev. & Tax. Code §63.2.

1) The rule in one paragraph

Parent-child exclusion (principal residence only). A child (or grandchild, if the middle generation is deceased) who receives a parent’s principal residence and makes it their own principal residence within 1 year may exclude the transfer from reassessment — but only up to the parent’s factored base year value plus an allowance ($1,044,586 for transfers 2025-02-16 → 2027-02-15; biennially adjusted by BOE). Market value above (factored base + allowance) is added to the factored base → partial reassessment. Rental property, second homes, vacation homes: NO exclusion — ever, at any price. “Carry-forward”: the parent’s factored base year value keeps its ≤2%/yr Prop 13 escalation; the allowance re-indexes every 2 years (Feb 16 of odd years) and applies as of the transfer date.

4) Carrying the allowance forward — why it is not “$1,044,586 forever”

  • The allowance is tied to the transfer date’s window, not to when the claim is filed. A 2045 transfer uses the 2045 biennial figure (est. ≈ $1,640,000–$1,830,000 depending on indexing) — the model shows low/high bands.
  • Two dates matter separately: (i) the transfer date sets the allowance and the 3-year claim clock; (ii) the FMV date (date of death) sets the delta.
  • Multiple children/one house: if the home passes to several children and one occupies, the exclusion applies to the whole property only if the occupying child receives ≥ the home (partial interests: the exclusion covers only the occupying child’s share pro rata). Buyouts of siblings after distribution are not parent-child transfers — the purchased shares are reassessed. Structure before signing anything.
  • Trust distribution mechanics: transfer at death (trust vests) — the 1-year occupancy clock runs from date of death, not from deed recording. The 3-year claim clock runs from the same date. Trustee should not record a deed that suggests a third-party transfer before the claim.
  • Move-out later: exclusion lost → full reassessment as of the lien date after move-out (with interest, sometimes escape assessments). Prop 19 is a residency contract, not a one-time gift.

6) Filing checklist (occupancy paths only)

  1. Day 0: gather decedent’s evidence (DL, voter registration, homeowners’ exemption on last bill, utility bills).
  2. ≤ 1 year: move in; file BOE-266 (homeowners’ exemption, $7,000 assessed-value reduction); update DL, voter registration, address with banks/insurers.
  3. ≤ 1 year: sign the pre-commitment acknowledgment that vacancy or double-residency collapses the exclusion.
  4. ≤ 3 years: file BOE-19-P with the Santa Clara County Assessor (not BOE). Attach: trust certification, death certificate, deed/APN, appraisal.
  5. On receipt of supplemental notice: verify taxable value = factored base + partial reassessment per §63.2(d); protest within ≈ 60 days if wrong.
  6. Annually: keep occupancy evidence current (renew BOE-266 if requested; utilities in child’s name).
  7. If any child moves out / A is rented / A sold to third party: notify assessor; expect reassessment; re-run the model.
Never claim Prop 19 unless the heir will occupy the home long-term. Filing while intending to rent “just for a year” voids the benefit retroactively in most implementations — the assessor will reassess with escape assessments and interest, and the $12,000–$19,000/yr savings evaporate with penalties. If occupancy is uncertain, do not file — sell at death instead.
