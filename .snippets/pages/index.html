The recommendation, in one paragraph

On today’s law and the figures available, the highest-certainty sequence is: (1) obtain a written date-of-death valuation; (2) confirm gross estate vs the $15,000,000 federal exclusion and file Form 706 for portability if a spouse survives; (3) if — and only if — the heir will genuinely occupy Property A long-term, claim Prop 19 (BOE-266 within 1 year; BOE-19-P within 3 years) and live there; otherwise sell in the same tax year as the inheritance, when step-up in basis (IRC §1014) makes pre-death appreciation ≈ tax-free; (4) park net proceeds in a 3M–2Y Treasury ladder for 24–36 months, then phase into a diversified equity allocation on the pre-agreed Investment Policy Statement — this phased route is the black-swan hedge (see Black Swan Lab); (5) keep Property B: it produces ≈ $85,800/yr with its owner unit freely available, and it is not an inheritance — evaluate it separately, including SF’s new Prop H parcel tax from July 2027. Renting Property A is not competitive once full reassessment applies — the math is on Property A. All of this reverses if condition 1 or 4 above flips — hence the Pre-Commitment Agreement first.

Property A vs Property B are not the same decision. A is inherited (step-up, Prop 19 tests, trust distribution). B is already owned (no step-up unless B itself is later inherited; existing tenants; Prop H now enacted; rent-control caps possibly binding). Every analysis on this site keeps them separate.

Top 12 actions, in order

  1. Write the decision criteria before the numbers exist — sign the Pre-Commitment Agreement (what would make each path win).
  2. Commission a written date-of-death appraisal of Property A (also sets Prop 13 reassessed value and estate inclusion).
  3. Ask now: portability and prior taxable gifts — Form 706/DSUE; $19,000 annual exclusion (2026); any 709s filed?
  4. Total the gross estate vs $15,000,000 (single, 2026) / $30,000,000 (couple) — Property A + B + cash + retirement + business interests + life insurance incidents of ownership.
  5. Model the sale in the same tax year as death explicitly (already built into Interactive Projections) before considering any hold.
  6. Get two written insurance quotes for A as owner-occupied (expect $5,000–$12,000/yr, not $1,500) and for B as 3-unit landlord (est. $2,000–$5,500).
  7. Put a market rent on A (≥2 independent estimates) — or accept that the letting paths are a floor, not a contender.
  8. Extract B’s leases + certificate of occupancy date — sets Prop H (from 2027) and rent-cap regime (SF 1.6% 2026-27 if pre-6/13/1979; else AB 1482 ≈ 8.8% SF-area 2026-27).
  9. Sign an Investment Policy Statement for the proceeds before the money arrives — including the phased Treasury→equity schedule and written black-swan conditions.
  10. Build the 5-year maintenance & capital plan for both buildings (roof, sewer, HVAC, seismic for B; solar/electrical for A).
  11. Un-round this model annually — re-run with updated Treasury rates, assessed values, rent caps, and new/proposed law (see Law Tracker).
  12. Decide, then write it down next to each item (this site logs decisions in Suggestions Review Board).
