Inheriting Property A through a trust — the process, in order

Applies to a California trust distribution of a single-family residence. Timeframes are typical, not legal guarantees. “Day 0” = date of death.

Phase 0 — Before anything happens (now, 2026)

  1. Confirm the trust’s terms (distribution clause for A; trustee powers; in terrorem clause; successor trustee identity), and collect the exact vesting deed.
  2. Write the pre-commitment letter (template) — decided when calm, not after the appraisal.
  3. Inventory everything (see Questions): real estate, cash, brokerage, retirement, business interests, life insurance (incidents of ownership), lifetime taxable gifts (Form 709 history), debts.

Phase 1 — Death through day 90

  1. Death certificate — order 6–10 certified copies (banks, county recorder, title, DMV, insurers).
  2. Trustee authority — successor trustee signs the Affidavit of Successor Trustee (Probate Code §13100 et seq.) to take title to A; no probate for trust assets. Record with Santa Clara County Recorder if a deed change is recorded.
  3. Secure and value A — insurance continuity check (vacant-home endorsement if empty), utilities, lawn/pool service; list contents; photograph for estate accounting.
  4. Date-of-death written appraisal (licensed appraiser) — sets (i) estate inclusion value, (ii) Prop 13 reassessed value under RTC §402.5, (iii) heir’s stepped-up basis (IRC §1014). Order immediately — appraisers are backlogged.
  5. Notify all trust beneficiaries (Probate Code §16061.7) within 60 days of death; keep proof of mailing; 120-day contest window runs from service.
  6. Open an estate/trust account; separate trustee fee agreement in writing (if any).

Phase 2 — Days 90–270 (tax windows open)

  1. File Form 706 within 9 months of death (+6-month extension via Form 4768) if gross estate exceeds the basic exclusion ($15,000,000 per decedent in 2026; indexed from 2027; DSUE portability needs a filed 706 even if no tax due). CA imposes no estate tax, and its former pick-up tax is $0.
  2. Form 709 for the decedent’s final gifts (if any) — same 9-month frame.
  3. California trust income tax: trust files Form 541 if income ≥ $100; choose fiscal vs calendar year; CA taxes capital gains as ordinary income.
  4. Property tax (Prop 19 clocks start): if any heir will occupy A — move in and file the homeowners’ exemption (BOE-266) within 1 year; file the parent-child exclusion claim (BOE-19-P) within 3 years of transfer (and before any third-party transfer). Late = exclusion lost or prospective-only.
  5. Supplemental assessment notice: expect a supplemental bill ~4–12 months after transfer (reassessment event). Protest deadline is on the notice — usually 60 days. Diarize on receipt.

Phase 3 — The decision point (day ~90–180)

Branch: (a) Sell A now — list, escrow, close; ≈ $3,410 county transfer tax + ~$60 recording + commissions; step-up ⇒ ≈ $0 gain; distribute cash per trust; reinvest per IPS (24–36-month Treasury ladder → phased equity entry). (b) Heir occupies A — move in ≤1 yr; BOE-266 + BOE-19-P; live ≥2 yrs; consider §121 on a later sale; do not rent any part of A or the exclusion collapses. (c) No occupancy, no sale — keep as investment: accept full reassessment (≈ $36,000/yr today), rent per AB 1482 caps, budget management; this is the weakest path on the numbers. Any mixed variant (e.g., live 2 years, then sell §121; or keep B, sell A) is modeled in Compare All Paths.

Phase 4 — First 24 months after transfer

  1. Final property-tax bills correct? Supplemental + prorated base year value reflect the appraisal?
  2. If Prop 19 granted — keep evidence: driver’s license, voter registration, utility bills at A.
  3. Insurance re-quote on the new risk profile (owner-occupied vs rental vs vacant).
  4. Reinvested proceeds: execute the ladder per IPS; set 6-month review; log benchmark vs policy.
  5. Update the household balance sheet; confirm estate/trust closed (trust distribution receipts signed).
  6. Calendar the annual model re-run and the quarterly law-tracker check (Law Tracker).

Phase 5 — Never-ending (the 50-year part)

  • Property tax: ≤2%/yr escalator (Prop 13) until next change of ownership; Prop 19 allowance re-indexes every 2 years (carry-forward already modeled).
  • Estate law: exclusion indexed from 2027; OBBBA permanence could still be legislated away by a future Congress.
  • Rent law: AB 1482 sunsets 2030-01-01 absent extension; SF Prop H runs 2027–2042, inflation-adjusted.
  • Black swans: annual stress review; keep the IPS honest; rebalance on schedule, not on feeling.
