Method, rounding, and the limits of this model

1. Privacy & placeholders

No personal names, addresses, APNs, account numbers, or documents appear anywhere on this site or in the repo. Property A = the inherited Cupertino home (ZIP 95014). Property B = the inheritor’s SF Outer Sunset home (ZIP 94122). The two are never conflated: A is inherited (step-up, Prop 19), B is owned (carryover basis, no step-up).

2. Rounding — nearest $10, by policy

  • Owner-supplied figures (bills, assessments, estimates) are republished rounded to the nearest $10 (exact source figures are never republished; a bill shown as e.g. $…,537 publishes as $…,540). This is deliberate: exact values would be traceable against public county records and are not needed for any decision here.
  • Computed figures (projections, taxes, net proceeds) are computed unrounded and displayed rounded to $10. Intermediate values may differ by ±$10 from unrounded chains; every table foots within that tolerance.
  • Statutory thresholds are exact (e.g., $15,000,000 estate basic exclusion; $1,000,000 BHSA threshold; $1,044,586 Prop 19 allowance; $250,000/$500,000 §121) — never rounded for display, because they are law.
  • Percentages are shown to 1–2 decimals; rates ≥ 1% shown to 2 decimals.

4. What this site deliberately does NOT model

  • Tax-return-exact computation — CPA territory; this site isolates the decision-relevant margins.
  • Credit/financing of the transfer — assumed no leverage at death; add if the trust borrows against A.
  • Health/longevity-contingent decisions — the live path depends on the heir’s actual life plans; no model substitutes for asking.
  • Behavioral drift — the IPS bands, decision log, and pre-commitment letter exist precisely because models don’t hold against emotion.
  • Tax law after 2026 — re-verified quarterly; this page decays by design.

5. Model risk disclosures

  1. Projections are illustrative compound models with user-set parameters; they are not predictions and not personalized advice.
  2. The Monte Carlo histogram assumes lognormal returns — real crashes have fatter tails; treat quantiles as ordinal (“which path is robust”), not cardinal (“ending value X”).
  3. Black-swan presets are deterministic drawdown overlays on a stochastic base — directionally honest, not statistically exact.
  4. Tax computations use 2026 law and 2026 thresholds; CA/federal law is a moving target (indexed annually; sunsets scheduled: AB 1482 2030-01-01; Prop H 2042-06-30; Prop 19 allowance re-indexes Feb 2027).
  5. Every error you find is a bug — log it via the Suggestions Review Board with sources; the maintainer fixes and re-verifies before merge. Do not hand-edit computed cells.
