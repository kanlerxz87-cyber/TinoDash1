Taxes & Law

Three separate systems are deliberately kept apart here, because conflating them is the most common planning error:

1 · Estate tax

Federal, on the transfer at death. California has none. Threshold = the exclusion amount.

2 · Income-tax basis

What the heir's cost basis is, and what gain is taxed when A is later sold — step-up at death, §121, §1031, depreciation recapture.

3 · Property-tax assessment

Prop 13 / Prop 19: the assessed value the county taxes each year — unrelated to income-tax basis.

Future projections — inheritance thresholds

The federal exclusion is indexed for inflation, so its nominal value in 2040–2050 will be higher than $15M — but the exact figure cannot be known and is therefore left blank by rule. What can be said:

  • Indexing historically moved the exclusion in steps of roughly 2–3% per year.
  • At ~2.5%/yr from 2026, $15M would reach roughly $22M–$28M by 2045 — a planning range, not a published value (clearly labeled as a projection, not a citation).
  • Any future legislation could change it in either direction; the tracker above is the control.
