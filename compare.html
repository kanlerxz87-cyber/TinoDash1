D. What the answer actually hinges on (ranked)

  1. Will the heir genuinely live in A, long-term? Yes → P1/P6. No → P3/P4 (or P5 if B occupancy attractive).
  2. Is there a defensible date-of-death value materially above/below $3.1M (today’s proxy)? Re-run projections — recommendation moves with it (breakeven bands on Projections).
  3. Gross estate > $15,000,000? If yes — Form 706, portability, possibly estate-liquidity planning before any distribution.
  4. Is entry timing risk acceptable? Yes → P3; No / 1929-2008 anxiety → P4 (ladder). Reinvestment risk page note: today’s 1–2Y yields ≈ 4.6%; if they halve, P4 still beats P3 in crashes by construction.
  5. Keep B regardless? Usually yes (imputed-rent arbitrage + $85,800 stable income + Prop H trivial) — decide B on its own merits, not to justify A.
Reading the grid honestly: the pure “rent A” path is dominated at 2026 law — full reassessment + thin cap rate + no exclusion. The real contest is P1/P6 (genuine occupancy) vs P4 (sell + phased ladder) vs P5 (sell A, live in/keep B). The Pre-Commitment Agreement forces this choice to be made deliberately, in writing, before the appraisal lands.
