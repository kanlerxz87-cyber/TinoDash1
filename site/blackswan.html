Black Swan Economic Conditions & Phased-Investing Stress Test

Growth and inflation are never constant over a 50-year horizon. This section tests a Phased-Investing Path (parking net sale proceeds in 3M–2Y U.S. Treasuries for 24–36 months after inheritance before phasing into diversified equities) against Lump-Sum Sell and Real Estate Hold across five historical/tail-risk regimes.

Reinvestment Risk Beside the Headline: Phasing through 3M–2Y Treasuries for 30 months dramatically softens 1929- and 2008-style Day-1 equity crash entry risk (and U.S. Treasury interest is exempt from California state income tax). However, in a normal bull market, staging carries an estimated ~$693,000 opportunity drag vs. Lump-Sum investing (2045 baseline, 15-yr horizon), plus T-bill rollover risk if short-term rates drop to 1%–2% during a recession.

Documented Investment Policy Statement (Write Before Money Arrives)

Agree on this written policy before inheritance so sale proceeds are deployed by rule rather than emotion:

1. Staging & Liquidity Ladder (Months 0–36)

  • Reserve 100% of estimated federal estate tax (if any) + closing/trust expenses in 3M–6M T-bills immediately.
  • Park remaining net proceeds in a 3M–2Y U.S. Treasury ladder (exempt from CA 13.3% state tax).
  • Automate monthly or quarterly equity tranches over 24–36 months (e.g., 1/30th per month), with an optional acceleration rule if the S&P 500 drops >20% from peak.

2. Target Long-Term Allocation & Rebalancing

  • Diversify away from Silicon Valley / Bay Area single-sector concentration (since Property B already provides $1.8M+ SF real estate exposure).
  • Target broad low-cost index allocation (e.g., 70% Global Equities / 30% Short-Intermediate Treasuries & CA Munis), rebalanced annually.
  • Review extreme Black Swan scenarios annually and update assumptions.
