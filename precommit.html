Pre-Commitment Agreement — Property A path selection

Purpose: prevent the outcome from being reverse-engineered from whichever projection happens to flatter the preferred path. Each path is chosen only if its triggers are met. Fill in, sign, date, witness. Review annually. This is a family governance document, not a legal contract — the estate attorney should bless it.

Part 1 — The triggers we agree on today

For each path, tick the box if the family agrees it is the right choice when its conditions hold. Specific quantitative triggers are pre-filled from this model — the family may tighten them, never loosen them, after signing.

Path L — “Live in Property A” wins if ALL hold:

  • The heir (or any one transferee) will genuinely occupy A as principal residence within 1 year of transfer and for ≥ 2 of the following 5 years;
  • The heir does NOT need to sell A within 24 months of death;
  • The heir accepts the ≈ $23,840+/yr (2026 terms) post-transfer property tax as the price of occupancy;
  • The heir files BOE-266 within 1 year and BOE-19-P within 3 years of transfer, or this path is voided;
  • Projected household satisfaction with Cupertino ≥ satisfaction with SF is acknowledged (lifestyle condition, not math).

Path R — “Rent out Property A” wins if ALL hold:

  • Condition L above fails (no occupant) AND the heir will NOT sell within 3 years;
  • Independent appraisal shows FMV at death < ≈ $2,400,000 (2026 $) — i.e., the Prop 19 full-carry regime applies (FMV ≤ factored base + allowance);
  • Independent market-rent ≥ ≈ $5,800/mo (2026 $, ≈ 2.25% of FMV/yr) AND rent growth ≥ 3%/yr for the hold;
  • Family acknowledges modeled cap rate ≈ 0.4–0.9% at current values and accepts it explicitly;
  • Gross estate ≤ exclusion and no liquidity need forcing sale.

Built-in default: at current figures, Path R does NOT qualify — it is the fallback for a property worth materially less, or for a family that refuses to sell for non-financial reasons. That is the honest pre-commitment: we have already decided, in advance, that renting loses on the numbers today.

Path S — “Sell in the same tax year as death” wins if ANY hold:

  • The heir will not occupy A (fails L) — and this is the default if L and R both fail;
  • Gross estate approaches/exceeds the exclusion and estate cash needs arise (§6166/2032A N/A) — sale proceeds fund estate obligations first;
  • Written appraisal comes in ≥ 15% above the owner’s estimate (≥ ≈ $3,565,000 2026 terms) — sell-into-strength;
  • Any co-beneficiary demands liquidation (no family agreement to hold) — sell, distribute cash, avoid forced partition;
  • Market conditions at transfer are orderly (no crash within the prior 6 months) — if crashed, route to Path P4 instead of Panic-Selling.

Path P4 — “Sell, park in a 3M–2Y Treasury ladder for 24–36 months, phase into equities” wins if ANY hold:

  • Path S conditions hold AND any of: (i) equity market within 15% of all-time-high at transfer; (ii) 1929/2008/2022-style signals flagged by the IPS review (yield-curve inversion > 3 months, credit spreads > 6%, CAPE > 35); (iii) the heir simply cannot tolerate −40% drawdown on the proceeds — chosen for sleep quality, explicitly;
  • Proceeds ≥ ≈ $5,000,000 — enough to make the 2–3%/yr ladder drag worthwhile vs single lump;
  • Reinvestment risk accepted: if 3M yields fall > 150 bp before tranche maturity, ladder extends ≤ 3 years total per IPS §4, no more.
